Settings

Riot ID

Your account name, so wins, losses and your champion show from your side. Got more than one account? Separate them with commas.

Replay folders

Where L-Recall looks for your replays (.rofl files). Folders inside these are checked too.

Game installs

Your League install. Every hour L-Recall checks it, and once Riot's finished updating, it saves a copy of the new patch.

Patch storage

Where saved patches are kept. Patches share a lot of their files, so saving the next one usually only adds a few GB.

Optional. Tidies the saved files so patches get ready faster. Takes a few minutes, and it's safe to stop halfway.

Watching replays

Before an old replay plays, its patch gets put back together from storage. That takes a minute or two, depending on your drive.

Recently used patches stay ready, so going back to one starts straight away. Each one ready uses about 25 to 30 GB.
So your latest replays start straight away. Only while you're not playing, and never in place of a patch you got ready yourself.
Only builds the champions that are in the replay, so a patch gets ready about twice as fast. If a replay doesn't start, it builds the rest and tries again by itself.
Your graphics, camera and hotkeys, copied from your League install each time a replay starts, instead of the game's defaults.

Champion & item icons

The little pictures in the replay list. Saved on your PC so they show up fast, even offline.

Riot Vanguard

Turn on Vanguard's Pre-Check if you can. It switches Vanguard on only when a game starts. Without it, Vanguard stays on all the time and stops replays from opening.

...

L-Recall only reads Vanguard's status. It never changes it or tries to get around it.

Updates

Checks GitHub for a newer version every now and then. Nothing about you is sent.

...

Uninstall

Removes L-Recall from this PC. Your replays are never touched, and saved patches stay unless you tick the box.
